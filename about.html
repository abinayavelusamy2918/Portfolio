01 — ABOUT

From systems engineer to program lead.

I started on the engineering side of the table — as a Systems Engineer at Tata Consultancy Services, then across contract and associate engineering roles at NTT DATA, Aspire Systems, and Cognizant. That background is still the lens I manage programs through: I read a roadmap the way I used to read a codebase — dependencies first, risk second, everything else after.

The shift into program leadership started at UBS as an Authorized Officer, then deepened through Temus and EPAM, where I ran governance for an AUD 2–3M, 45+ person integration program end to end. Today, at Purands, I own the roadmap for an AI-driven customer engagement platform — translating strategy into shipped releases for enterprise and mid-market clients.

Alongside delivery, I completed an MBA at Singapore Management University (Dean's Lister, Executive Leadership Committee), the NUS Future Leader Programme, and Microsoft Azure Fundamentals — and I'm currently in IIT Madras's Professional Program in Agentic AI and Applications.

I still build. Prototypes, side projects and the occasional agent — partly to stay close to what I'm asking engineering teams for, partly because some problems are easier shown than specified. Most of it is on GitHub.

Three jobs, one intersection.

Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom

Most people treat program, product and project management as one job with three names. They aren't — and each fails differently.

  • Product decides what is worth building.
  • Project decides how it gets delivered.
  • Program decides what it costs and what it risks.
  • The core is where all three agree.

I built this to make the argument spatially. The core isn't a fourth discipline — it's the small region where the three overlap, and it's the only place a release ships on time, in scope, and worth having. Most of my work is keeping that intersection from collapsing.

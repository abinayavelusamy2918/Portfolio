CASE STUDY — UBS GOLD

Trading gold from a banking app.

A standalone gold-trading product for Swiss-domiciled clients, rebuilt as a native feature inside UBS Mobile Banking — so buying physical gold sat alongside the client's accounts instead of in an app they had to remember to open.

CSAT 4.5/5+ ~CHF 3M annual revenue 20K+ users 6+ releases · 12+ sprints
// THE_PROBLEM

A good product in the wrong place.

Swiss-domiciled clients could buy, sell and hold physical gold through a standalone application. The proposition was sound — gold is a core part of a Swiss private-banking relationship — but the packaging worked against it.

01

Gold lived outside the app clients used daily, so it was out of sight at the moment they were reviewing their portfolio.

02

A separate app meant a separate login, a separate onboarding path, and its own release train to maintain.

03

Holdings didn't sit next to the client's other positions, so gold read as a side product rather than part of the portfolio.

04

Two codebases and two release cycles for one proposition — cost the bank carried with no client benefit.

// PRODUCT_STRATEGY

Meet the client where they already are.

The strategic call was to stop treating gold as a product with its own front door and treat it as a capability of the banking app. Three principles drove the decisions that followed:

PRINCIPLE 01

Zero new friction

No second login, no second onboarding. If a client is authenticated in Mobile Banking, they are authenticated for gold.

PRINCIPLE 02

Gold as a position

Holdings, live CHF pricing and performance presented in the same visual language as every other asset the client holds.

PRINCIPLE 03

One release train

Ship inside the bank's existing mobile release cycle rather than maintaining a parallel one.

// ROADMAP

Sequenced by risk, not by feature list.

A regulated banking app doesn't allow a big-bang cutover. The roadmap deliberately front-loaded the riskiest unknowns — authentication and pricing integration — so that if they failed, they failed early and cheaply.

R1

Discovery & feasibility

Mapped the standalone journeys against the banking app's existing patterns, and confirmed with engineering what could reuse the bank's authentication and pricing services.

R2

Authentication & entitlement

The hardest dependency first: only Swiss-domiciled, entitled clients see the feature at all. Getting this wrong is a compliance issue, not a bug.

R3

Read-only vault

Holdings and live CHF pricing shipped before any transactional capability — real client value with a fraction of the risk surface.

R4

Buy & sell

Transactional flows added once the read path was proven stable in production.

R5

Decommission

Standalone app retired only after parity was demonstrated, so no client lost access mid-migration.

// DESIGN

Borrowed patterns, not new ones.

Design worked from the bank's existing mobile design system rather than porting the standalone app's interface. Every screen was assembled from components clients already knew, which removed the need to teach anything new and shortened accessibility and brand review.

Flows were prototyped in Figma and walked through with compliance, front-office and engineering in the same room — deliberately, because a gold trade has regulatory constraints that surface faster in conversation than in a document. What emerged: an entry point inside the client's portfolio view, a vault screen showing holdings against live CHF pricing and performance history, and buy/sell flows that reused the bank's existing confirmation and audit patterns.

// DELIVERY

Shipped in slices, inside the bank's cadence.

Delivered as Product Owner across Agile and hybrid sprints, feeding into the Mobile Banking portfolio's existing release train. I owned the backlog and the release sequencing, converting requirements into shipped increments and running RAID across the migration — the dependency on the bank's authentication service and the pricing feed were the two risks tracked hardest, because either could stop a release.

Governance updates, release status and risk summaries went to senior stakeholders every cycle. Over the programme this scaled into governance across 8 agile crews in the wider Mobile Banking portfolio.

// OUTCOME

Gold Vault shipped successfully into UBS Mobile Banking and the standalone app was retired. The banking application it became part of sustained CSAT above 4.5/5 and an estimated annual revenue contribution exceeding CHF 3M, with digital banking releases in this portfolio reaching 20K+ users.

Figures are portfolio-level for the digital banking application this feature became part of, not attributable to Gold Vault alone.
